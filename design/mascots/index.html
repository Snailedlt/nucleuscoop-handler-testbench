Duo and Nucli

One mascot for splitscreen.me, one for the handler tools. Both grow out of the splitscreen.me logo: Duo is its split blue window; Nucli is a quiet, helpful atom whose electrons are the players. One colour scheme each, the same in light and dark. The motion borrows BSManager's tricks: a wind-up before every move, squash and stretch, overshoot, and a pause before the spin.

Players: split and merge

One mascot per player. Duo splits off a new friend when a player joins and two of them crash back into one when a player leaves; Nucli pops an electron out, or swallows one. Each player can be present (a controller Nucleus can see), assigned (placed on a screen, holding a controller) or active (their game is running). Try the buttons, or click a mascot. Several clicks in a row run faster. Press 1 to 8 on your keyboard to press a button as that player.

1 player
  • Present Nucleus sees the device. Duo looks around; Nucli's electron is hollow.
  • Assigned Placed on a screen. Duo holds the player's device, ready; the electron fills in.
  • Active Their game is running. The Duos hop to a shared beat; the electron speeds up with a trail. Everyone active is a party, and even Nucli gets excited.
  • Device The small icon next to each player: controller, keyboard and mouse, keyboard only, or mouse only. Duo holds it and Nucli's electron takes its shape; a new device pops in with a jiggle.
  • Input When a player presses something, their Duo hops and their device and electron jiggle, like a haptic tap.

Explorations, round 3: in the buttons, in every look

Ideas only, nothing here is in the product. Copy, save, undo and the rest get their animation inside their own button, where your eyes already are: 0.3 to 0.6 s, cut off by the next click, the electron colours as accents. Then everything in the workbench's four looks, light and dark, with the Motion setting.

Animations in the buttons

  • Copy: the front sheet stamps onto the back one, a check draws on the copy, the label says "Copied" for 1.6 s.
  • Save: the arrow drops into the tray, which squashes as it lands.
  • Undo, redo: the arrow swings back (or on) and springs; an electron flashes at its tip. 0.34 s, because undo comes in bursts: each press restarts it.
  • Format: the lines snap into their indentation, one after another.
  • Fix all: a flick of the wand, the spark turns, three electrons fly off.
  • Share: the link's two halves click together with a ring; "Link copied".
  • Try it: the play button presses in and pops; an electron runs once round it.
  • With less motion, Motion "Still" or "Off", or the alpha 8 look: no movement, only the state (the check, the label).

Every look, light and dark

The buttons in each pane work too. "Still" here shows each mascot's designed still pose; the workbench freezes the current frame instead, and the new loaders start from an empty frame, so that should change with them.

What I see

  1. The mascots hold up in every look: their own colours don't depend on the theme, and the panels, tracks and code lines take the look's colours.
  2. v3 (monochrome) is the one where they stand out most. If that's too loud, the simpler drawings at lower saturation would sit better there.
  3. Player colours: the looks use their own (#ff6b35, #3b8bff, #ffcf3a, #3ddb86), Nucli uses the splitscreen.me button colours. Tick the box to compare: one set of player colours everywhere would be clearer. Mine: the look's, since players see them in the setup too.
  4. alpha 8 and Motion "Off" fall back to plain icons and text; nothing is missing.
  5. "Still" should show the designed still pose (no animation) instead of freezing a frame.

Explorations, round 2: time first, simpler drawings

Ideas only, nothing here is in the product. An animation only works if it fits the moment it is on screen, and if you're looking there. So first how long each moment lasts in the workbench (from its code, and timings I measured with the core), then round 1 judged against that, the ones I reworked, simpler drawings, and colours again (violet only).

How long each moment is on screen

What that means
  1. The animation takes at most a third of the moment; the rest is a still pose you can read.
  2. Every frame works as a still. A toast closes, a run ends, a page reloads: he can be cut off at any point.
  3. Never react to a keystroke. React to an action (Try it, Fix, Copy) or to something that stays, 1.5 s after you stop typing.
  4. Animate where the eyes already are. If Nucli isn't there, the feedback goes on the button or the line, without him.
  5. Loaders by wait: nothing under 0.4 s (it only flashes), a spinner up to 2 s, then progress, and past 10 s also how long it has been.

Simpler drawings, the same animations

Flat fills, one shadow shape, no gloss, rim light, status light or blush, and eyes as dots with one glint. Duo drops to about half his shapes; Nucli loses fewer (his electrons and orbits are most of him), but his body and face go from 35 shapes to 14. Easier to edit, lighter to run, cleaner at 16 px. The parts keep their names, so every animation in the library runs on them as it is: the detail is in the timing, not in the layers.

Colours, round 2: violet only

Teal and gold are out. Six violets on the refined shape, each in a toast and the empty Check tab, light and dark.

My recommendation

  1. Simpler drawings for both, small builds at 24 px and under. Keep the detail in the motion.
  2. Build first: the loader by wait (it replaces every spinner), startup at 0.8 s, wake-up, escalating per run.
  3. Ultraviolet if you want him livelier, Indigo if you want him calmer. Both beat today's in a toast.
  4. Drop undo, copy, and hover; move the big fix to its toast and Duo's juggling to the game launch.

How to use them on a website or in an app

On a website

  • As an image, the simplest: <img src="duo-joy.svg" alt="Duo cheering" width="120" height="120">. The SVG animates, and stands still by itself when the visitor asks for less motion.
  • A GIF needs a still for reduced motion: <picture><source srcset="duo-joy-still.svg" media="(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)"><img src="duo-joy.gif" alt="…"></picture>.
  • Decoration next to text that says it: alt="". Loaders: the text gets role="status", the image alt=""; under ~32 px use the twirl or the spinner.
  • Inline (to restyle with CSS): paste the file's <svg>; each carries its own <style>. Give each copy unique ids.
  • The web kit's example.html shows all of these.

In an app

  • Discord: Server Settings → Emoji → Upload Emoji: the PNGs as static emoji, the GIFs as animated ones. Avatars for profile pictures.
  • WinForms (like Nucleus Co-op): a PictureBox with the PNG, or the GIF (it animates). WPF / Avalonia: the SVG through SharpVectors or Svg.Skia, or the PNGs.
  • Electron, Tauri, web views: as on a website. Android: the still SVG as a Vector Asset, GIFs through Glide or Coil. iOS / macOS: PNGs or the still SVG in an asset catalog.
  • Games (Unity, Godot): the 512 px PNGs as sprites; Godot imports SVG too.
  • Wherever the system has a "reduce motion" setting, show the still file then.

Keep them right

Duo's stripe stays white and vertical (a cross for four players), Nucli's electrons in the player colours. Scale evenly; don't stretch, recolour the body or add outlines.

Open it on its own page

A concept for player setup in a future Nucleus Co-op. Only the devices you actually use show up: a controller when you press one of its buttons, the keyboard when you press a key, the mouse when it moves, the touchscreen when you touch it (a browser can't tell two keyboards or two mice apart). No controller to hand? Add a pretend device under Devices: it joins, moves and leaves like a real one. Press A on a controller to join, or Join on a device. Every controller gets its own cursor, so several people can set up at the same time. The screen splits for each new player and merges when one leaves. Move devices by dragging them, or pick one up (Enter or A) and put it somewhere else. Everything works with a mouse, touch, the keyboard (arrows, Enter, Esc, Delete) or a controller (D-pad or stick, A, B, X, Start). Under the screen, each player is a Duo holding their devices: Duo splits off a new one when someone joins, they merge when someone leaves, and a Duo reacts when its player presses something. On a controller: A hops, B ducks, X wiggles, Y cheers, Start gets everyone hopping, and the D-pad or stick makes him look and lean that way.
